What WordSnap does with your text

Effective 2026-09-04. Applies to the WordSnap Chrome extension, version 0.1.

WordSnap runs in your browser and has no server. This page lists what the extension reads, where it sends it, what it keeps on your machine, and why it asks for each permission.

What the extension handles

The text of a draft you choose to analyze: the content of the compose window on a supported site at the moment you click the WordSnap badge or press Re-analyze. If you turn on auto-analyze in settings (off by default), analysis also starts on its own once a draft passes 40 words and again after edits.

WordSnap does not read other messages, your inbox, your contacts, or any page you are not composing in. It never reads a draft you have not asked it to analyze.

Where it goes

To the language model provider you configured, under your own account and that provider's privacy terms:

  • OpenRouter (default), connected with one click. OpenRouter privacy policy.
  • Anthropic, with your own API key. Anthropic privacy policy.

The fact check and counterargument passes use the provider's web search. Claims extracted from your draft are sent as search queries through that provider. Sources shown in the extension come from those search results and nowhere else.

No copy of your text goes to WordSnap. There is no WordSnap server in this version.

What is stored on your machine

  • Your settings, including the provider choice and the auto-analyze switch.
  • Your OpenRouter key or Anthropic API key, in the extension's local storage (chrome.storage.local). It is never placed in synced storage and is readable only by the extension's background process, never by a web page or by the script that draws over your draft.
  • A cache of claim verdicts, keyed by the claim text, kept for 24 hours so an unchanged claim is not verified twice.
  • Session state for each open compose window: the findings for that draft, until the tab or the compose window closes.

What is not collected

  • No WordSnap account. There is nothing to sign up for.
  • No analytics, no telemetry, no usage tracking.
  • No crash or error reporting. Errors are logged to your browser's console only.
  • No cookies and no identifiers of any kind sent to WordSnap.

Permissions and why

PermissionWhy it is requested
storageKeep your settings, your key and the 24 hour claim cache on your machine.
activeTabReach the compose window in the tab you are working in when you click the extension.
identityOpen the OpenRouter sign-in window for the one-click connection. Nothing else uses it.
scriptingLoad WordSnap into a page that is not in the list below, and only when you click the extension icon on that page.
mail.google.comDraw over the Gmail compose window.
x.com, twitter.comDraw over the X post composer.
www.linkedin.comDraw over the LinkedIn post and message composers.
openrouter.aiSend analysis requests to OpenRouter.
api.anthropic.comSend analysis requests to Anthropic when you use your own key.
https://*/* (optional)Asked for only when you click the icon on a site outside the list, so WordSnap can work with a text area there. You can decline, and it is never requested on its own.

How to delete your data

Remove the key on the settings page, or uninstall the extension; both delete everything WordSnap stored. The claim cache clears itself after 24 hours. Anything already sent to your provider is governed by that provider's retention policy and your account with them.

Open source

The full source is at github.com/jalemieux/wordsnap under the Apache-2.0 license, so every statement on this page can be checked against the code.

Changes and contact

If a future version adds a WordSnap-operated service, this policy will be rewritten and the effective date above will change. Questions go to GitHub issues or privacy@wordsnap.ai.
